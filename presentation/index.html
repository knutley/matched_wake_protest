Matched wake analysis · worked example

A matched pair in the UK

Does police attention in an area change after a right-coded protest, compared with a left-coded protest held under the same conditions? Matched wake analysis answers this by pairing protests and counting what happens around each one. This page goes through one pair.

The two protests and every event around them are illustrative. They are generated to show how the method works, not taken from ACLED. The pooled estimates in the last section are the paper's.

1. Two protests, matched

Protest A is right-coded; protest B is left-coded. They are in different cities and months, but coarsened exact matching puts them in the same stratum: same country and year, both on a Saturday, a similar distance from the nearest government building, and a similar number of other protests nationally that day.

A filled dot is one where police were present, which is the outcome. Hollow rings are police-attended protests before the matched protest, and the small grey dots are protests with no police reported.

Event density is exaggerated so the wakes are visible. In the real data, a 4 km wake usually holds well under one police-attended protest a month, which is why the method pools thousands of pairs.

3. Counting before and after

For each protest, compare police-attended protests after it with those before it. The question is whether the change after A differs from the change after B. Using the before-period count controls for areas that simply have more policed protest to begin with.

One pair is noisy. Across all matched pairs, the paper estimates

Yposti = α + δ·Yprei + τ·Ti + εi

where Ti = 1 for a right-coded protest. τ is the difference-in-differences: how much the after-count around right-coded protests differs from that around matched left-coded protests, given the same before-count. It is reported as a percentage of the average after-count around left-coded protests.

4. From one pair to all of them

Pooled across France, Germany, Italy, Spain and the UK (2020–2024), police-attended protests within 4 km of a right-coded protest are about 30% below the level after a matched left-coded protest in the first week, and still about 15% below after three months. As many protests follow either kind; police attend fewer of those that follow right-coded protests.

Point estimates with 95% confidence intervals, 4 km, from the working draft of 26 October 2026. Each window counts days 1 to t. Per follow-up protest, the rate ratios are 0.71, 0.79 and 0.83.

From Katelyn Nutley, “Does Partisan Bias Become Institutionalised? Repeated Exposure and the Policing of Right-Coded Protest”, working draft. The method follows Schutte and Donnay (2014).
